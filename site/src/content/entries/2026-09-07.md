To witness suffering—whether it arrives as flood, heat, conflict, or the veneration of those who committed atrocity—is to be confronted with a kind of spiritual vertigo. The question that emerges from a day like this is not whether we can fix all that is broken, but whether we have the courage to remember truly, to refuse amnesia, and to let that remembrance humble us into solidarity with those who cannot speak. There is a prayer in bearing witness without looking away.
