The preacher who wrote Ecclesiastes knew that human history moves in seasons, and that the human desire to impose permanent solutions on temporary moments often deepens the wound. Today's news carries the weight of that old wisdom: strategic calculations that seemed calculated weeks ago now reverberate; institutions strain under pressure; movements that promised order produce displacement and protest. There is no moral calculus offered here—only the observation that we live in a time when many are acting, few are listening, and the consequences ripple outward in ways no one fully intended. What might it mean to pause and ask not whether our cause is just, but whether this moment calls for action or for the harder work of restraint?
