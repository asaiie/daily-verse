The news carries accounts of those most vulnerable—the drowned, the displaced, the imprisoned, the unborn in crisis—alongside our collective uncertainty about how to respond to their presence. This passage does not ask whether we agree on policy or on who bears responsibility; it asks whether we see the hungry, the thirsty, the stranger, and the captive at all. Some of today's headlines suggest we do: there is a reckoning with historical injustice, a scientist mentoring a young dreamer, a community investigating why its children are born too soon. Others suggest we have turned away. The verse offers no comfort to those who wish to remain distant; it invites each reader to ask what it means to recognize Christ in the suffering and abandoned we encounter, or choose not to encounter, each day.
