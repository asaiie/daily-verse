When institutions crack and trust fractures, when those meant to lead appear at odds and those meant to serve struggle under the weight of systems failing them, the temptation is to see only dissolution. Yet the prophet Jeremiah, writing amid the rubble of Jerusalem's fall, reached not for certainty or vindication but for a single anchor: that mercies are new each morning, not because circumstances have changed, but because God's faithfulness remains constant. In seasons when public confidence shakes and the powerful falter, there is an invitation to look beneath the headlines for small renewals—a healthcare worker still willing to serve, athletes still gathering to honor their dead, ordinary people still feeding their families and caring for neighbors. This is not denial of the real fractures we see. It is the long view that has always sustained those who wait.
