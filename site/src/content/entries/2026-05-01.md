The prophet's cry in Lamentations—that remembrance of affliction brings bitterness—meets a day when news carries stories of those held without clarity, families grieving, and millions facing hunger and displacement. Yet Lamentations does not stop at despair; it turns toward hope precisely because the speaker refuses to look away from suffering. We are invited to sit with the ache of a world where power often obscures truth, where the vulnerable wait without answers, and where cruelty persists. The ancient text asks whether we have the courage to lament honestly—to name what breaks hearts—before we reach for easy comfort. In such honesty lies the beginning of mercy.
