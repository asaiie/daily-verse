Today's headlines carry the weight of accumulated grief—the person sheltering in wreckage, the family displaced from home, the child lost in sudden collapse, the migrant in transit. Lamentations is the voice of those who cry out not for judgment but for witness, asking if anyone sees what has been lost. The verse does not promise swift resolution or explain why suffering falls where it does; it simply insists that sorrow deserves to be seen, that pain does not disappear when we look away. On a day when so many carry fresh wounds, the ancient words invite us not to offer answers but to look steadily, to acknowledge that what is broken matters, and to sit with the reality that much suffering in this world remains unredressed and unfair.
