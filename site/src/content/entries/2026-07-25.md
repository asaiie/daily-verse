When the news carries multiple stories of sudden loss, chronic illness without support, and the daily struggle to secure basic nutrition, the instinct is often to look away or to demand swift justice. Yet the writer of Lamentations offers something different—not denial of suffering, but a willingness to hold it fully in mind while still turning toward mercy as the deeper reality. The affliction is real, the vulnerability is not imagined away, and yet the witness persists in noticing that we are not consumed, that mercies are renewed each day. On a day like this, that gentle insistence—that new mercies arrive each morning, without our earning them—may be the only honest word available.
