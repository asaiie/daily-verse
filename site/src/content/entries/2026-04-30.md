The Psalmist wrote this not from a place of calm but from inside the noise — earthquakes, floods, nations in uproar. The claim is not that trouble will stop, but that something holds when it doesn't. Today's headlines carry that same texture: markets lurching, communities picking through wreckage, institutions wobbling under the weight of mistrust. Into all of it, this old line drops like an anchor: not a promise of rescue on demand, but a declaration that the ground beneath the ground is still there. "A very present help" — not distant, not eventual, but present, already in the trouble with you.
