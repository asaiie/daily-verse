When we survey a day's worth of news—new infrastructure approved, algorithms trained to diagnose, disputes resolved through courts, aid blocked by conflict, institutions strained and reformed—we encounter the old human predicament: our certainty about the right path often outpaces our wisdom about its destination. A pipeline expansion, a military operation, a technological deployment, a political decision—each can appear rational, even necessary, in the moment. Yet history teaches that the consequences of our choices often unfold far from where we stand, touching lives we did not imagine and creating conditions we did not foresee. The verse does not counsel paralysis or despair, but rather a kind of epistemic humility: an invitation to hold our convictions lightly enough to remain attentive to voices we have not heard, outcomes we have not considered, and the vulnerability of those we may not see.
