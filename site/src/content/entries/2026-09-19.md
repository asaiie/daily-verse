When systems fail those without power to protect themselves—the detained, the imprisoned, the displaced, those whose histories lie scattered and unguarded—the cry rises not as accusation but as anguish. The psalmist speaks from confinement and helplessness, naming God as refuge not because rescue is guaranteed, but because there remains someone to whom suffering can be spoken truly. Today's news carries many such cries: from those in cells, from those separated by force, from those trying to preserve what the world indifferently destroys. To read such headlines is to be invited not into judgment but into solidarity with lament—to acknowledge that there are people today for whom the land of the living feels very much like captivity, and to hold before God their vulnerability without rushing to resolution.
