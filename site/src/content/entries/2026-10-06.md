The psalmist's cry reaches across centuries when we encounter a world where trust is weaponized, where the young are conscripted by force, where the poor and fleeing are caught in the machinery of conflict. These are not abstract wrongs but ruptures in the fabric of human dignity—a spy betraying his country, children torn from safety, civilians caught in strikes meant for others. The question posed in Lamentations invites us not to turn away but to witness, to let sorrow do its work in us, to recognize that such suffering demands not explanation but presence, not vindication but grief.
