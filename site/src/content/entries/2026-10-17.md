The news carries the weight of those in the low places—families who lost loved ones to senseless violence, communities targeted for who they are, survivors of historical atrocities still waiting for justice, and those caught between the machinery of power and survival. Lamentations speaks not from the heights of authority but from the dungeon, where a voice cries out not with certainty of answer but with the raw need to be heard. In such times, we might ask whether our systems—political, legal, social—have ears that truly listen to the vulnerable, or only to those who can afford to amplify their voices. The promise here is not that suffering ends, but that there is one who draws near to those who call, who has not hidden his ear from the breathing of the desperate.
