When hunger claims lives in one place, violence erupts in another, and the displaced face uncertain futures across many borders, we are reminded that vulnerability is not abstract but lived—felt by real people in real moments. The psalmist does not promise that the humble will be spared suffering, but that their cry is heard, and that God's concern turns toward those least able to defend themselves. Today's world presses this truth upon us: the fatherless, the oppressed, those without resources or protection, remain the ones for whom justice and mercy matter most. In a day crowded with power plays and institutional disputes, we might ask ourselves whether our gaze has settled where it ought—on the small, the suffering, the ones whom systems too often overlook.
