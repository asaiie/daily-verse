When justice stalls, when rescue remains uncertain, when wounds from decades past refuse to close, we are tempted to believe that suffering has the final word. Yet the ancient book of Lamentations was written precisely in such conditions—in the wreckage of loss and abandonment—and it testifies to a mercy that persists even when human systems fail. The verse does not deny grief or suggest that pain will vanish; rather, it holds both truths at once: that sorrow is real and that it does not exhaust the nature of things. Today's headlines show us people trapped in various kinds of waiting—legal, physical, historical—and invite us to consider what it means to trust in a compassion that operates on a longer timeline than our own desperation.
