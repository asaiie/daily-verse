The voice of Lamentations speaks not from judgment but from the depths of suffering—a cry that refuses to look away from pain, even when it seems easier to pass by. Today's news carries the weight of many afflictions: the grinding destruction of infrastructure and lives in zones of conflict, the sudden collapse that traps families in rubble, the cold that threatens children, the gunshot that ends a morning at a gym. These are not moments for explanations or abstractions, but for the hard work of witnessing—of staying present to sorrow rather than moving past it. In such hours, perhaps the most faithful response is not to defend or debate, but to look closely at what has been broken and to ask what it means to stand alongside those who carry such grief.
