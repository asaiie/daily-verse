On days when headlines speak of diplomatic deadlock, technological advancement outpacing safeguards, natural disaster, and violence that persists despite human effort to prevent it, the Preacher's words cut close to the bone. We see elaborate systems—climate negotiations, international courts, autonomous weapons treaties—strain under the weight of competing interests and shifting circumstances, reminding us that neither cleverness nor speed nor strength guarantees the outcomes we seek. This is not cause for despair but for a quieter realism: perhaps the work of justice and protection is not primarily about getting ahead, but about fidelity to what we can actually do, and mercy toward what we cannot control. The question then becomes not whether we will win, but whether we will show up, attend to the vulnerable, and refuse to harden our hearts while time and chance unfold.
