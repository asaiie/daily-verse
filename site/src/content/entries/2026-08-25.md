The cry of Lamentations is not a question expecting comfort; it is a summons to witness suffering that might otherwise be passed by unremarked. Today's news carries the weight of many such cries—children caught in epidemics, families displaced by weather, captives held in darkness, refugees still fleeing after years of persecution. The ancient voice asks us to pause and truly see, not to explain away or resolve quickly, but to let the reality of others' sorrow enter us. To pass by such witness unmoved is itself a kind of turning away that faith calls us to resist.
