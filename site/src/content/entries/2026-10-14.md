When the news carries stories of illness, displacement, sudden death, and the grinding anxiety of those whose work may disappear, we are invited to sit with suffering rather than explain it away. The apostle Paul does not promise that tribulation will vanish, but that God's comfort is real and relational—it reaches us in our own griefs so that we might become vessels of that same comfort for others. Perhaps the deepest response to a day like this is not to solve every problem, but to notice where we ourselves have been comforted, and to ask where we might offer that same tender presence to those who bear burdens we cannot fully see.
