The news today speaks of displacement, confinement, courtroom verdicts for old atrocities, and new violence that scatters both lives and ancient artifacts across hostile terrain. When we hold together the weight of what is happening—the scale of suffering, the slowness of accountability, the helplessness of so many caught in circumstances they did not choose—it can feel as though affliction has the final word. Yet the writer of Lamentations knew this particular darkness and still found reason to speak of mercies that are new each morning, not because the pain has ended, but because the Lord's compassions do not fail. To recall affliction in remembrance is not denial; it is the beginning of hope that is honest, and therefore real.
