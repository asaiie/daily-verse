On a day marked by mass gatherings and mass suffering—crowds seeking spiritual anchor, others displaced by flood or caught in the machinery of conflict—Micah's ancient question rings with particular weight. The prophet does not ask for grand gestures or certainty about outcomes, but for three practices that ground us in the real: justice that sees the actual person in front of us, mercy that does not wait for permission to act, and the humility to remember that our understanding is always partial. When the headlines show systems straining under the weight of human need—health systems collapsing, vulnerable populations caught between forces beyond their control, disease spreading among the marginal—we are invited to consider what justice, mercy, and humility might mean in the small circle where we have actual power to act.
