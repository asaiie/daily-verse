On days when the news carries so much anguish—outbreaks that overwhelm, disasters that claim lives, vulnerable people displaced or abandoned—it can feel as though sorrow has the last word. Yet Lamentations does not ask us to deny the weight of suffering; it asks us to remember it fully, to let it humble us, and in that honest reckoning to find something that endures. The act of witnessing suffering, of not turning away from it, is itself a kind of faithfulness. In a world where so much pain goes unacknowledged, we are invited to hold these afflictions in remembrance, not in despair, but as a step toward a hope that has looked suffering in the face and chosen still to look forward.
