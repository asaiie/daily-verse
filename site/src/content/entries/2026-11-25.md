The preacher's ancient observation echoes across a modern day shaped by tariffs and market swings, by the constant churn of consumer desire, and by the restless pursuit of advantage. We build, buy, secure, and yet something in the architecture of these efforts leaves us unsettled—not because effort is wrong, but because effort alone, however frantic, cannot anchor what seeks to remain unfixed. The question is not whether to cease building or choosing or competing, but whether we might notice, as we do these necessary things, that the trembling we feel points toward a hunger that none of them were ever meant to satisfy.
