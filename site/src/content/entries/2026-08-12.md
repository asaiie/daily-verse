When the ground itself becomes unstable and rescue workers labor through the night to pull the living from rubble, when waters swallow the unprepared, when threats ripple through infrastructure and borders, we are reminded of how little we control and how much we depend. The verse from Lamentations speaks not of a world made safe but of mercies that persist within a world of genuine peril—a faithfulness renewed each morning not because danger has passed, but because we are still here to meet it. In such times, the appropriate posture is neither optimism nor despair, but a kind of vigilant gratitude: that we wake, that others are found alive, that compassion has not failed us yet.
