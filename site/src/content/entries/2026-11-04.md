Today's headlines show leaders and movements surprised by outcomes they did not anticipate—some by the electorate's judgment, others by their allies' stumbles. The verse speaks not of punishment inflicted by God, but of consequences that follow from a failure to see clearly what others can perceive. When we move through the world convinced of our own direction, we may miss the small warnings, the shifts in sentiment, the costs of neglecting what matters most to those we seek to lead. Prudence is not passivity; it is the hard work of paying attention, of asking whether the path we've chosen is truly serving those we say we serve.
