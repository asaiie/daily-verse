On a day when the news carries reports of violence, evictions, conflicts renewed, and the vulnerable pushed further to the margins, the psalmist's promise arrives not as reassurance but as acknowledgment: brokenheartedness is witnessed. There is no promise here that suffering will be quickly resolved or that the powerful will suddenly relent, but rather that closeness itself—divine attentiveness to those whose hearts are fractured—constitutes a form of salvation. The verse invites us to consider what it means to remain present to suffering without rushing to fix it, and to hold the possibility that nearness, in itself, is not nothing.
