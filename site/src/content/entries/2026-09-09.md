The ancient voice of Lamentations calls us to witness suffering without turning away—to see it, to acknowledge it, to let it matter. Today's landscape holds many forms of sorrow: the measles deaths among children who had no voice in their own protection, mothers whose bodies lack what their unborn need, families displaced by storms and conflict, communities fractured along old and new lines of division. These griefs do not fit neatly into narrative arcs of progress or blame; they simply are, and they ask something of those who hear them. To read such news with eyes open is to resist the comfortable distance that allows us to pass by, and to sit instead with the reality that the vulnerable often pay the steepest price when systems fracture and competing interests collide.
