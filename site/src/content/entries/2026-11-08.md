When sudden violence and tragedy strike—whether in crowded squares, on railway carriages, or in distant conflicts—the immediate response is often numbness and unanswerable questions. Yet the book of Lamentations, born from profound communal suffering, does not offer easy comfort; instead, it insists that grief itself is a form of witness, and that even in desolation, mercy persists. The verse reminds us that God's compassion is not suspended when tragedy visits us, but rather meets us in the midst of it. To sit with those who mourn, to acknowledge the weight of loss without rushing past it, and to hold space for the mysterious persistence of mercy even when it cannot be felt—this may be the only honest response to days when so many wake to shattered lives.
