The voice of Lamentations is not one that rushes to answers or offers easy comfort. It is a voice that stops and stares, that asks whether we truly see what is before us. When news of mass casualties, failed safeguards, and abandoned protections accumulates across a single day, the ancient cry seems neither distant nor archaic—it asks us to pause, to witness, to acknowledge the weight of suffering that others carry. Lament is not despair; it is the necessary act of seeing clearly, of refusing to turn away, of sitting with the broken and the bereaved long enough to honor their loss rather than explain it away.
