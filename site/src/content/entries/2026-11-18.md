The psalmist's cry assumes that complaint itself is a form of prayer—that bringing our troubles into the presence of God matters, even when no one else sees or hears. Today's headlines reveal suffering that happens largely beyond public view: battles in tunnels, detentions without trial, the daily toll of addiction, the quiet displacement of those with no voice in policy rooms. We live in an age of competing narratives and misplaced credit, where the most urgent human crises often remain hidden or disputed. Yet the act of witnessing, of acknowledging what is truly happening beneath the headlines, may itself be a form of faithfulness—a refusal to let the hidden remain only hidden, a willingness to pour out complaint and truth, however uncomfortable, before the one who sees all.
