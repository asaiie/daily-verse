The news today carries the weight of fragility—sudden death, displacement, rising costs of care, the vulnerability of those we love. In such moments, the temptation is to look for assurance that all will be made right. But Lamentations does not offer that comfort; it offers something deeper: a witness to mercy that persists not as a guarantee against suffering, but as a presence within it. The verse does not deny the reality of loss or hardship; it acknowledges them while naming that each morning brings not exemption from trouble, but the possibility of renewed compassion. When families strain under caregiving costs, when cities live under the threat of violence, when the displaced long for ordinary life—these are the mornings in which we are invited to recognize mercy not as a solution, but as a faithfulness that continues. This is an invitation to lament together, and to notice, each day, that we have not been abandoned to despair.
