The verse does not ask whether the stranger deserves welcome, or whether the sick are citizens, or whether caring for the vulnerable serves our interests. It simply establishes a measure of faithfulness: do we see human need and respond? Today's news presents that challenge in its starkest form—people crossing borders in desperation, children without legal voice, communities watching their homes disappear, the sick multiplying faster than medicine can reach them. We are invited not to resolve these crises from a distance, but to ask what it means to recognize the hungry, the stranger, the naked, the sick in our own time, and whether our systems and choices reflect that recognition or diminish it.
