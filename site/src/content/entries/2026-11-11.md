The psalmist's simple act—pouring out complaint, naming trouble aloud—carries particular weight on a day when so many grievances press from different quarters: the unhoused and precarious, those caught in transit disasters, families in unstable nations, communities vulnerable to threats both real and rumored. There is wisdom in the refusal to paper over anxiety or to move too quickly past lament. Before solutions come counsel, before answers come the hard work of articulating what is broken, what hurts, what makes us afraid. The psalm does not promise that complaint alone fixes anything, but it insists that the troubled are heard, that naming difficulty is itself a form of fidelity. On such a day, we might ask ourselves: to whom do we turn when the weight grows heavy, and do we make space for others to do the same?
