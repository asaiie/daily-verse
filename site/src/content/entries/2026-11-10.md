The preacher's observation—that there is a time for tearing apart and a time for stitching together—asks us to consider which moment we inhabit and what it requires of us. We cannot force the seasons; we can only attend to them honestly. When peace talks begin and accidents claim lives, when investigations proceed and creative work persists, we are reminded that history does not move in a single direction, and that both the wounds and the slow work of repair are real. Perhaps the question for today is not whether resolution is possible, but whether we can remain present to whatever season is unfolding—awake to both the breaking and the mending.
