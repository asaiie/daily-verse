When the day's news speaks of thousands fleeing their homes, of vulnerable children torn from their families, of hunger that touches billions, and of conflicts that seem to multiply rather than resolve, it is tempting to turn away in despair. Yet the voice in Lamentations does not counsel forgetting or numbness; it insists on remembering affliction fully, letting it humble the soul rather than harden it. To recall suffering—to sit with it rather than escape it—is paradoxically where hope begins, not as false comfort but as clarity. In witnessing displacement, trafficking, hunger, and violence without flinching, we refuse the comfort of indifference and remain positioned to extend mercy to those caught in these currents.
