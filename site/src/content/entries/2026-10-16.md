The psalmist writes as one who has watched the vulnerable suffer without recourse—the fatherless, the oppressed, those with no advocate—and cries out that God has not forgotten them. Today's reports of children killed, minorities hunted, voters disenfranchised, and the marginalized made subjects of medical trials without consent echo this ancient plea. The verse does not offer swift vindication or easy answers about why such things occur; it offers instead the harder comfort of being heard, of having one's cry witnessed by a God whose attention turns specifically toward those the world has cast aside. In a day dominated by the suffering of those without power to defend themselves, perhaps the only honest posture is to join the psalmist in that desperate hope: that to cry out is not to cry into silence.
