When the news carries reports of sudden, senseless loss—of lives taken in moments of violence, of systems failing the vulnerable, of conflicts deepening—we are tempted either to turn away or to demand swift judgment and solutions. But Lamentations invites us into something different: the permission to grieve fully while holding onto a harder, stranger hope—that mercy persists even when suffering is real and present. The verse does not deny the grief or suggest it will pass quickly; it acknowledges that sorrow is real, yet speaks of a compassion that does not abandon us in it. On days like this, perhaps our first work is not to analyze or assign blame, but to sit with the weight of loss, to name the broken places, and to ask where we might offer the mercy we ourselves have received.
