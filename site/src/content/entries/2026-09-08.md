When the news carries reports of conflict, theft, suffering, and displacement across continents, it is easy to feel the weight of a world in which power competes against itself and the vulnerable suffer in the margins. Yet the voice in Lamentations does not turn away from this reality—it names the affliction, holds it, and then pivots, not to denial, but to a steadier recognition: that mercies are renewed each morning, that faithfulness persists even when institutions fail, and that hope is not naive optimism but a choice to trust in what endures beneath the breaking. The invitation today is to mourn what needs mourning, and then to notice, in the space of a new day, where compassion and resilience still show themselves.
