There is a particular pain in days when the news arrives heavy with suffering—when children are killed, when the powerless bear the costs of decisions made far from them, when justice systems fail, when medical harm goes unmeasured and uncounted. The prophet's voice in Lamentations does not rush past this weight or offer cheap comfort; he sits with the taste of it, names it fully, lets it humble him. Yet he does not stop there. In the very act of remembering affliction, he finds something like a foothold: the capacity to recall, to witness, to refuse to look away. This is not hope as denial, but hope as a choice to keep one's eyes open and one's heart alive even when the world breaks. What does it mean, on a day like this, to remember with intention, to let sorrow teach us something, and to choose, despite everything, not to turn to stone?
