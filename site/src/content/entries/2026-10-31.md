When the world presents us with images of displacement, disease, and institutional strain—when families are separated by circumstance and conflict, when health systems strain under unprecedented outbreaks, when communities face the long, slow work of rebuilding—it is tempting to read these as signs of abandonment. Yet the writer of Lamentations knew something about devastation and refused despair not by denying suffering but by recalibrating what faithfulness looks like in its midst. The mercies the psalmist names are not the absence of trouble but their renewal: each morning offers a fresh opportunity to tend to the displaced, to resource the vulnerable, to rebuild what was lost. Today's news asks us to consider whether we, too, can meet each dawn not with resignation but with the kind of attentiveness that sees in small reunions and ongoing relief efforts the shape of mercy still at work.
