On days when the news carries the weight of so much suffering—the trapped, the sick, the displaced, those crying out in detention cells—we are invited not to look away but to look deeper, holding lament and hope together. The prophet does not pretend the affliction is not real or that remembering it is wrong; instead, he insists that memory itself, when held before God, becomes the ground of hope. To witness today's sorrows without hardening our hearts, to name them, to let them humble us: this too is prayer. In the rubble and the silence and the overwhelming need, something in the human spirit—battered but not broken—still reaches toward mercy.
