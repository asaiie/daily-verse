When we read of those who survive what should have been unsurvivable, those trapped in cities stripped of water and light, those whose words are silenced, and those mourning catastrophic loss, we find ourselves in the territory of lament—not a giving up, but a clear-eyed acknowledgment of the weight we carry. The writer of Lamentations knew this weight intimately, and rather than turning away from the pain, he held it in full view while allowing a slender thread of hope to emerge: not because the suffering ceases, but because mercy itself is new each morning. To remember affliction with humility is not despair; it is the posture that makes us capable of receiving the compassion that sustains us when our own systems fail.
