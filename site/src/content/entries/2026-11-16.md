The world's response to those on the margins—the migrant seeking asylum, the person in prison, the victim of circumstance or tragedy—sits at the heart of what we claim to believe. These verses ask us not for judgment of the vulnerable, but for recognition of Christ in them. When systems of control, detention, and removal dominate the headlines, we might pause to consider whether our policies and our indifference reflect the actual shape of mercy, or only its performance. The question is not whether others deserve compassion, but whether we recognize that in our treatment of the least, we are answering to something far greater than ourselves.
