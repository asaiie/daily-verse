When disease spreads faster than medicine can follow, when waters claim whole families, when those meant to heal are themselves harmed—the weight of these days invites us to ask what comfort means in the face of such scale and swiftness. The verse does not promise the suffering away, but names a God described as the Father of mercies, and invites us to consider that those who have themselves been held in dark hours may be the most honest witnesses to others' pain. To comfort is not to explain away tragedy, but to stand near it, and to carry forward the mercies we have received. In such a moment, where might we offer presence rather than platitude?
