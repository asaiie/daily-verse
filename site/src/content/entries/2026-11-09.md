When the news carries so much suffering—sudden tragedy, deliberate harm, the grinding vulnerability of those without enough to eat—it can feel as though the world runs on indifference. Yet the book of Lamentations teaches us that honest witnessing of affliction, without rushing past it toward comfort, is itself a spiritual practice. To remember what happened, to let it humble us, to refuse the easy lie that everything is fine—this is not despair but a kind of faithfulness. Hope, the text suggests, does not erase what we have seen; it grows *from* the hard work of truly seeing it. On days like this, we are called simply to remember, to grieve, and to remain open to what faithfulness asks of us.
