The litany of harm across today's headlines—a teenager's hand lost in a street confrontation, a ship captain killed carrying grain, a child wounded by airstrikes, journalists detained without cause—invites us into the posture of lament rather than explanation. Jeremiah's ancient cry over the ruins of Jerusalem speaks across centuries to anyone who has watched suffering unfold and felt the inadequacy of words. There is something necessary in pausing to simply grieve what has been broken, to let the weight of it settle, before asking what comes next. In a world where we are often pressed to assign blame or extract meaning, lament offers a different kind of honesty: the acknowledgment that some things should not have happened, that loss is real, and that bearing witness to it is itself a form of faith.
