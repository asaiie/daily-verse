The voice in Lamentations speaks not in judgment but in anguish—calling out to the indifferent, asking if anyone truly sees the weight of what is happening. Today's news carries echoes of that cry: those killed in conflict, families fleeing storms, survivors naming long-hidden abuse, people caught between survival and dignity. The verse does not demand answers or swift solutions; it names the reality that suffering exists and asks whether we will look away or truly behold it. To behold is not yet to fix, but it is to refuse the comfort of unseeing. In a day marked by accumulating loss, the ancient lament offers permission to grieve without immediately reaching for explanation.
