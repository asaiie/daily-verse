When violence scatters across headlines—drone strikes, train collisions, mass shootings—the temptation is to read the world as governed by chaos or judgment. Yet the scripture reminds us that affliction is not God's native language; compassion is. Today's news also holds glimpses of this mercy at work: communities building free childcare systems, disability rights activists inheriting a legacy of care, international aid flowing toward suffering regions. These are not naive answers to violence, but faithful insistence that human hands—moved by what the scripture calls the multitude of God's mercies—can still reach toward the vulnerable. To read today's news is to sit with both the grief that is real and the knowledge that suffering is never God's final word.
