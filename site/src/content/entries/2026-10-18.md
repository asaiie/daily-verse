The voice of Lamentations is not one that rushes to explanation or comfort, but one that names what is happening and asks whether anyone is truly seeing it. Today's news carries echoes of that ancient cry—violence in the streets, hunger at the margins, the targeted persecution of vulnerable people, the spiraling of addiction, families displaced and afraid. The passage does not demand that we have answers or assign blame, only that we look, truly look, at the suffering around us and acknowledge its weight. In a moment when it is easy to turn away or reduce tragedy to headline, the invitation is simply to behold—to let the reality of others' pain matter to our vision of the world.
