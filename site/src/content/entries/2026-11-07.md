When institutions stumble—elections fragment, medicines fail to heal, banking systems trap the vulnerable, violence claims the innocent—we confront the limits of human order and competence. The news today reflects a world where systems we rely upon disappoint us, where the suffering are multiplied, where certainty dissolves. Yet the author of Lamentations writes not from a place of denial but from the pit of genuine sorrow, and finds there a strange mercy: that we wake each day not because we have earned it, but because compassion is renewed. This is not a comfort that erases loss or excuses failure. Rather, it invites us to notice what persists despite collapse—care offered in crisis, young voices still calling for dignity, the stubborn human impulse to bear witness and remember. In a day fractured by many griefs, there may be something worth holding: that faithfulness is not a reward for the fortunate, but a gift that meets us precisely where systems have failed.
