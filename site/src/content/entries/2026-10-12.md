When agreements crumble and the vulnerable bear the weight of broken covenants, the psalmist's cry becomes our own: *For the oppression of the poor, for the sighing of the needy.* Today's news is a tapestry of those caught in the gap between promises made and promises kept—infrastructure struck despite pledges of restraint, journalists detained, newborns denied protective medicine, voters disenfranchised, families displaced. The Lord's response in the psalm is not a statement of comfort but of recognition: He *hears* the sighing. He *sees* the oppression. Whether we believe such a God exists or acts may depend on what we do when we hear the sighing ourselves—whether we, too, will arise.
