There is a particular kind of weariness that comes from watching suffering persist year after year, crisis upon crisis—wars grinding on, disease spreading, families trapped, lives suspended. The book of Lamentations does not offer false comfort or quick resolution; it sits in the rubble and asks hard questions. Yet even in Jeremiah's darkest meditation, a thread emerges: the recognition that suffering is not the final word, that compassion coexists with grief in God's nature. To hold both truths—that real anguish fills the world and that mercy is not exhausted—may be where hope lives today, not in the promise that all will soon be well, but in the stubborn refusal to believe that cruelty or indifference is all that moves the universe.
