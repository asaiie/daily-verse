When the news carries reports of earth shaking beneath people's feet, of hunger spreading through regions where crops have failed, of fires advancing on homes and forests, we are reminded of how fragile our hold on safety truly is. The ancient writer of Lamentations knew this fragility—knew what it meant to witness loss that seems overwhelming, to live in a world where disaster comes without warning. Yet even in that dark hour, the writer discovered something: that each dawn brings not judgment or abandonment, but a renewal of mercy. The verse invites us not to dismiss the real suffering around us, but to hold it alongside a deeper truth—that we are held by faithfulness that does not sleep, that shows up again tomorrow, and the day after that.
